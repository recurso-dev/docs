You don't need an accounting background to run Recurso — but ten minutes
here will make every number in the product explainable. This page teaches
the model from scratch, using the exact postings Recurso makes.

## The problem bookkeeping solves

Imagine tracking money with a single `balance` column. Every bug is
invisible: if the number is wrong, nothing tells you *where* it went wrong.

Double-entry bookkeeping is the fix, and it's an engineer's fix: an
**append-only event log with an invariant**. Money is never created or
destroyed in the records — it only *moves between named buckets*, and every
movement records both ends. If the two ends ever disagree, you know
immediately, and you can replay the log to find out where.

## Accounts are buckets with a sign convention

An **account** is a named bucket. Recurso provisions these automatically:

| Code | Account | Type | What it holds |
|------|---------|------|---------------|
| `1000` | Cash | asset | Money you've received |
| `1100` | Accounts Receivable | asset | Money customers owe you |
| `2100` | Deferred Revenue | liability | Subscription money you've billed but **not yet earned** |
| `2200` | Tax Payable | liability | Tax you collected and owe the government |
| `2300` | Customer Credit | liability | Wallet balances and credit you owe customers |
| `4000` | Revenue | revenue | One-off income, earned immediately |
| `4100` | Recognized Revenue | revenue | Subscription income, earned month by month |
| `5000` | Refunds | expense | Money returned to customers |

(The [full chart](/advanced/ledger#built-in-account-codes) adds TDS
Receivable, Credits & Adjustments, and Bad Debt Expense.)

Two intuitions carry you a long way:

- **Assets** are what you *have or are owed*. **Liabilities** are what you
  *owe someone else* — including "a month of service the customer already
  paid for."
- **Debit** and **credit** are not "minus" and "plus." They're the two ends
  of a transfer: debit = where value flows **to**, credit = where it flows
  **from**. Debiting Cash means cash went *up*; crediting a liability means
  what you owe went *up*. The signs feel inverted until you stop reading
  them as arithmetic and start reading them as **direction**.

## A posting is a transfer

Recurso's ledger rows look like this — one debit account, one credit
account, one amount:

```json
{
  "debit_account_id": "…AR…",
  "credit_account_id": "…Deferred…",
  "amount": 499900,
  "code": 1,
  "reference_id": "…the invoice…"
}
```

That's the entire mental model: **every financial event is one or more
transfers between buckets**, each tagged with a `code` saying what kind of
event produced it and a `reference_id` pointing at the business object.

The invariant falls out for free: since every row adds the same amount to
one side and the other, **total debits always equal total credits**.
Checking that is called a **trial balance**, and Recurso asserts it
continuously — in CI on every commit, and in your books on demand.

## One subscription month, end to end

A customer subscribes at ₹4,999/month + 18% GST (₹899.82). Watch the
buckets — amounts in minor units:

**1. Invoice issued** (Code 1 — gross, ₹5,898.82):

```
Debit   1100 Accounts Receivable   589882    "the customer owes us"
Credit  2100 Deferred Revenue      589882    "we owe them a month of service"
```

**2. Tax carved out** (Code 6 — the GST isn't yours; move it to its own debt):

```
Debit   2100 Deferred Revenue       89982
Credit  2200 Tax Payable            89982    "we owe the government this"
```

**3. Payment received** (Code 3):

```
Debit   1000 Cash                  589882    "money arrived"
Credit  1100 Accounts Receivable   589882    "they no longer owe us"
```

**4. Revenue recognized** (Code 2 — the month elapses; you earned it):

```
Debit   2100 Deferred Revenue      499900
Credit  4100 Recognized Revenue    499900    "now it's really income"
```

After all four transfers:

| Account | Balance | Meaning |
|---------|---------|---------|
| Cash | +589,882 | You hold the money |
| Accounts Receivable | 0 | Nothing outstanding |
| Deferred Revenue | 0 | The service was delivered |
| Tax Payable | +89,982 | Remit this at filing time |
| Recognized Revenue | +499,900 | This month's earned income |

Debits = credits at every step. Nothing appeared or vanished — it moved,
and the log says exactly when and why.

## Why bother deferring revenue?

Because "we got paid" and "we earned it" are different events, and serious
finance (and ASC 606) insists you not confuse them. If a customer prepays a
year and cancels in month two, the un-earned ten months were never your
income — they were a liability you owed back. Deferral makes refunds,
audits, and month-end close *boring*, which is the goal.

One-off charges skip this: a setup fee is earned the moment it's billed, so
its Code-1 entry credits `4000 Revenue` directly.

## Reconciliation: proving the log and reality agree

Balanced books can still be *wrong* — an invoice could exist with no
postings at all, and debits would still equal credits. So Recurso runs a
second check: [reconciliation](/advanced/ledger#reconciliation) walks every
billing record (invoices, payments, credits, wallets, write-offs) and
verifies its postings exist and sum correctly — about twenty distinct
checks, down to the minor unit. The number you want is
`total_discrepancies: 0`, and the [quickstart ends by showing you exactly
that](/quickstart).
